# Purpose

This chapter defines governed directions for identity, organization, and access evolution while preserving enduring security, privacy, tenant, and accountability invariants.

---

# Evolution Principle

Identity evolution should reduce friction and standing privilege while improving proof, privacy, portability, revocation, and explainability.

New technology cannot make identity infallible or turn authentication into authorization.

---

# Evolution Horizons

## Horizon 1 — Governed Foundation

* Unified identity taxonomy
* Organization and membership lifecycle
* Access packages
* Workforce and customer journeys
* Service and agent identities
* Delegated and privileged access
* Federation and recovery
* Access reviews and evidence

## Horizon 2 — Contextual Enterprise Access

* Richer relationship and resource context
* Continuous session and workload assurance
* Automated joiner-mover-leaver orchestration
* More precise just-in-time access
* Partner and developer federation
* Improved consent and identity portability

## Horizon 3 — Privacy-Preserving Federation

* Selective attribute disclosure
* Portable verifiable claims
* Cross-organization trust frameworks
* Federated agent and workload identity
* Regional and sovereign identity profiles
* Privacy-preserving proofing and analytics

## Horizon 4 — Adaptive Zero-Standing-Privilege Ecosystem

* On-demand scoped authority
* Continuous but bounded risk response
* Federated capability delegation
* Automated revocation and remediation
* Human-governed machine-to-machine ecosystems

Horizons are maturity states, not fixed dates.

---

# Adaptive Access

Future access engines may adjust challenge, duration, field visibility, approval, monitoring, or transaction limits using approved risk evidence.

Adaptive decisions must remain explainable, testable, versioned, observable, reversible, privacy appropriate, and unable to discriminate through prohibited attributes or ungoverned proxies.

Risk scoring is decision context, not an identity or permanent reputation.

---

# Portable and Verifiable Claims

Future ecosystems may allow an actor to present portable claims about identity, organization, qualification, device, workload, or authority.

The receiving service validates issuer, audience, freshness, revocation, purpose, minimum disclosure, and local policy. A valid claim does not guarantee access.

---

# Agent and Machine Ecosystems

Agents and workloads may operate across organizational and provider boundaries through federated identities and capabilities.

Federation requires accountable sponsors, constrained tools, tenant scope, purpose, budget, chain of delegation, evidence, revocation, and exit. Self-asserted agent identity or model signature is insufficient.

---

# Decentralized Administration

More administration may move to tenant, partner, project, and resource owners through policy-constrained self-service.

Decentralization increases the need for clear scopes, safe defaults, separation of duties, review, evidence, monitoring, and automatic expiry.

---

# Identity Intelligence

AI may help identify stale access, explain decisions, recommend packages, summarize evidence, detect anomalies, or propose remediation.

AI cannot independently prove identity, approve privilege, override policy, merge identities, or make irreversible employment, customer, security, or legal decisions.

---

# Research and Promotion

New proofing, biometric, behavioral, risk, credential, or federation capabilities begin in controlled research using synthetic or approved data. Promotion requires necessity, proportionality, accessibility, bias evaluation, privacy, security, interoperability, operations, recovery, and exit.

---

# Enduring Invariants

* Identity is not authority.
* Authorization is explicit, current, contextual, and provider enforced.
* Tenant and resource boundaries remain end to end.
* Delegation is bounded and revocable.
* Privilege is exceptional and attributable.
* People and non-human actors use distinct identities.
* Recovery does not weaken proofing.
* Consent and identity evidence remain purpose bound.
* Automation remains governed and reversible.
