# Purpose

This document defines the canonical knowledge architecture and lifecycle for XeniosAI.

It establishes how knowledge sources, artifacts, items, versions, classifications, provenance, approvals, publications, representations, citations, corrections, supersession, suspension, archival, and deletion are modeled and governed.

The chapter defines:

* Knowledge ownership
* Knowledge domains
* Source identity
* Artifact identity
* Knowledge-item identity
* Versioning
* Authority classification
* Temporal validity
* Provenance and lineage
* Lifecycle states
* Lifecycle transitions
* Review and approval
* Publication and activation
* Change and supersession
* Suspension and withdrawal
* Archival and deletion
* Derived-representation lifecycle
* Tenant and property behavior
* Governance and evidence

Detailed acquisition, extraction, normalization, chunking, enrichment, and human-curation pipelines are defined in ARCH-016-03.

# Knowledge Principle

Knowledge is a governed, source-attributable information asset made available for an approved purpose.

Content does not become active knowledge merely because it:

* Exists in a file
* Was uploaded
* Was extracted
* Was indexed
* Was embedded
* Appears relevant
* Was generated by AI
* Was used previously

Knowledge becomes active only through an explicit lifecycle decision appropriate to its authority, risk, scope, and use.

---

* Representation references

# Artifact Version

An Artifact Version is immutable.

A change to source content should create a new version when it changes:

* Meaning
* Scope
* Effective period
* Authority
* Classification
* Citation location
* Rights
* Integrity

Minor technical reacquisition of identical content may reuse the logical artifact version while recording a new acquisition event.

* Source authentication

* Acquisition evidence

* Backup integrity

# Authority Classification

Knowledge authority classes include:

## Authoritative Reference

Approved source for a defined knowledge subject.

## Approved Guidance

Reviewed guidance suitable for defined operational or user support purposes.

## Informational

Useful information without authority to determine policy or business truth.

## Draft

Content under development and not approved for general production use.

## Derived

Content created through transformation, extraction, summarization, translation, or inference.

## External Unverified

External content whose correctness or authority has not been independently established.

## Historical

Content retained for historical, evidence, or comparison purposes and not current guidance.

---

* Authority class
* Subject
* Scope

* Approval basis
* Limitations
* Review date

Authority must not be inferred only from source popularity, retrieval frequency, search rank, or prior model use.

# Scope

Knowledge scope may be:

* Public
* Enterprise
* Tenant
* Property
* Team
* Role
* Individual
* Explicitly shared

Scope is separate from authority.

Content may be authoritative within a property while inaccessible outside that property.

# Tenant Scope

Every non-public Source, Artifact, Knowledge Item, Representation, Citation, Lifecycle Record, and Evidence Record should identify tenant scope.

Tenant scope must be established through authenticated context and source registration.

Tenant identifiers embedded in content must not override trusted scope.

---

# Property Scope

Property-specific knowledge should identify:

* Property
* Tenant
* Local owner
* Effective period
* Relationship to tenant-wide knowledge
* Allowed override
* Local review
* Local withdrawal

No property may publish content into another property's scope without explicit authority.

---

# Inheritance

Knowledge may be inherited from:

* Enterprise to tenant
* Tenant to property

Inheritance should define:

* Parent item or policy
* Child scope
* Effective version
* Allowed variation
* Conflict rule
* Review behavior
* Supersession behavior
* Citation behavior

Inheritance must not create hidden copies that no longer follow parent lifecycle.

# Material Knowledge Change

A new Knowledge-Item Version is required when changing:

* Meaning
* Instruction
* Authority
* Scope
* Classification
* Effective period
* Source basis
* Policy interpretation
* Material citation
* Required action
* Warning

Correcting non-semantic formatting may not require a new logical version but should remain evidenced.

---

# Version Immutability

A published Knowledge-Item Version should be immutable.

Correction creates:

* A new version
* A supersession or correction relationship
* A lifecycle action for the prior version
* Representation updates
* Citation handling
* Evidence

Silent editing of a published version is prohibited.

---

# Version Coexistence

Multiple versions may coexist for:

* Future-effective policy
* Historical reference
* Tenant migration
* Property migration
* Language transition
* Consumer compatibility
* Legal evidence

Retrieval must distinguish current eligible versions from historical or future versions.

---

# Temporal Model

Knowledge time should distinguish:

* Source event time
* Source publication time
* Acquisition time
* Processing time
* Approval time
* Publication time
* Effective start
* Effective end
* Review due
* Supersession time
* Withdrawal time
* Archival time
* Deletion time

The latest processed version is not necessarily the currently effective version.

---

# Future-Effective Knowledge

Future-effective knowledge may be published before it becomes active.

It should:

* Identify effective start
* Remain excluded from current-use retrieval by default
* Be discoverable to authorized planners
* Preserve current version
* Support scheduled activation
* Produce activation evidence

---

# Retrospective Knowledge

Historical or retrospective queries may request the knowledge effective at a prior time.

Such retrieval should define:

* Historical purpose
* Query time
* Scope
* Authorization
* Available version history
* Known gaps
* Citation

Current knowledge must not be substituted silently for historical knowledge.

# Proposed

Proposed identifies a source, artifact, or item requested for inclusion.

It should record:

* Proposer
* Purpose
* Domain
* Expected owner
* Scope
* Intended consumers
* Initial risk

Proposed knowledge is not production retrievable.

---

# Registered

Registered means identity, ownership, scope, and lifecycle metadata have been established.

Registration does not establish:

* Content integrity
* Approval
* Authority
* Publication
* Retrieval eligibility

---

# Acquired

Acquired means an artifact version has been received or referenced from its registered source.

Acquisition records:

* Source version
* Acquisition method
* Time
* Scope
* Integrity metadata
* Contract outcome

Acquired content is not automatically validated or active.

---

# Quarantined

Quarantined content is isolated due to:

* Source failure
* Integrity failure
* Malware or unsafe content
* Invalid format
* Missing metadata
* Scope ambiguity
* Policy concern
* Rights concern
* Classification uncertainty

Quarantined content must not enter production retrieval.

---

# Validated

Validated means required structural, source, integrity, scope, and contract checks succeeded.

Validation does not necessarily establish:

* Semantic correctness
* Authority
* Approval
* Currentness

---

# Classified

Classified means required:

* Knowledge domain
* Authority class
* Sensitivity
* Tenant
* Property
* AI-use eligibility
* Retention
* Residency

have been assigned or confirmed.

---

# Curated

Curated means required content and metadata quality activities are complete.

Curation may include:

* Structural correction
* Metadata correction
* Taxonomy
* Conflict resolution
* Summary validation
* Citation review
* Language review

---

# Under Review

Under Review means an authorized reviewer is evaluating:

* Purpose
* Accuracy
* Authority
* Scope
* Classification
* Effective period
* Quality
* Risk
* Consumer use

Under-review content is excluded from general production use unless an explicit policy allows a restricted use.

---

# Approved

Approved means the exact Knowledge-Item Version has received the required accountable decisions.

Approval should identify:

* Approver
* Authority
* Scope
* Decision
* Conditions
* Effective period
* Review due
* Evidence

Approval is not publication.

---

# Published

Published means an approved or otherwise eligible Knowledge-Item Version has been made available to defined retrieval channels.

Publication establishes:

* Eligible consumers
* Scope
* Retrieval channels
* Citation behavior
* Effective-time behavior
* Representation requirements
* Monitoring

Published future-effective or restricted content may not yet be active for general current use.

---

# Active

Active means the version is currently eligible for its approved production purpose.

Active eligibility requires:

* Valid lifecycle
* Effective time
* Valid source status
* Required representations
* Access policy
* No active suspension
* Sufficient freshness

---

# Suspended

Suspended temporarily removes or restricts use due to:

* Accuracy concern
* Security concern
* Privacy concern
* Legal concern
* Source incident
* Quality issue
* Scope error
* Expired assurance
* Operational risk

Suspension must propagate promptly to retrieval and derived representations.

---

# Superseded

Superseded means a newer version or item replaces the prior version for defined use.

Supersession should identify:

* Replacement
* Effective time
* Scope
* Reason
* Citation behavior
* Historical availability
* Consumer notification

Superseded knowledge may remain available for authorized historical use.

---

# Expired

Expired means the approved validity or freshness period ended.

Expired content should be excluded from current-use retrieval unless policy explicitly permits:

* Historical use
* Evidence use
* Review use
* Temporary continuity with warning

---

# Withdrawn

Withdrawn means the source owner or governing authority has removed the knowledge from permitted use.

Withdrawal may require:

* Immediate retrieval exclusion
* Consumer notification
* Context-cache invalidation
* Representation invalidation
* Incident review
* Evidence preservation

---

# Archived

Archived content is retained outside ordinary current-use retrieval for:

* History
* Audit
* Legal obligation
* Dispute
* Research under approved policy
* Migration evidence

Archive access must remain authorized and tenant scoped.

---

# Deleted

Deleted means the knowledge content is no longer available for ordinary processing and has entered the governed deletion state.

Deletion should address:

* Artifact
* Knowledge item
* Representations
* Citations
* Caches
* Search results
* Replicas
* Exports
* Backups
* Evidence

Evidence may retain minimal deletion facts without retaining deleted content.

* Guest-facing retrieval
* AI Orchestrator retrieval

* Workflow-task retrieval

# Activation

Activation determines current-use eligibility.

Activation may occur:

* Immediately on publication
* At a scheduled effective time
* After representation readiness
* After tenant approval
* After property readiness
* After operational validation

---

# Representation Readiness

Publication should define required representations.

Examples:

* Canonical content
* Metadata
* Lexical index
* Semantic representation
* Citation map
* Language variant

If a required representation is unavailable, the item should not be active for the affected channel.

Optional representation failure may trigger a defined degraded mode.

* Domain change
* Correction

* Tenant request
* Property change
* Legal change
* Security incident
* Consumer feedback

# Change Impact

* Affected contexts
* Affected tenants

# Correction

Correction should:

* Identify the incorrect version
* Identify the correct source
* Create a new version
* Record correction reason
* Preserve required history
* Supersede or suspend the prior version
* Rebuild representations
* Invalidate caches
* Update citations
* Notify affected consumers where required

Silent correction of published content is prohibited.

---

# Urgent Correction

Urgent correction may be required for:

* Safety
* Security
* Privacy
* Legal obligation
* Material guest harm
* Material operational error

Urgent response may first suspend the affected item, then publish a reviewed correction through an expedited governed process.

Urgency must not bypass tenant or property isolation.

---

# Supersession Model

Supersession may be:

* Full
* Partial
* Scope specific
* Tenant specific
* Property specific
* Language specific
* Time specific

The relationship should identify which uses move to the replacement and which historical uses remain valid.

---

# Citation after Supersession

Existing citations to a superseded version should remain resolvable for authorized historical evidence.

Citation resolution should indicate:

* Superseded status
* Replacement
* Effective time
* Current-use warning
* Access policy

Citations should not silently redirect in a way that alters historical evidence.

---

* Graph
* Curated answers

* Citation resolver

# Retention

Knowledge retention should consider:

* Business purpose
* Legal obligation
* Contract
* Historical value
* Evidence
* Rights
* Tenant requirement
* Property requirement
* Personal data
* Security sensitivity

# Archival

Archival should:

* Remove content from ordinary retrieval
* Preserve identity and provenance
* Preserve tenant and property
* Apply archive access
* Preserve required citations
* Maintain integrity
* Define retrieval for authorized historical use
* Define retention and disposal

Archive is not an uncontrolled fallback search index.

---

# Deletion

* Artifact storage
* Knowledge Registry
* Metadata
* Lexical index
* Vector index
* Graph
* Summaries
* Translations
* Caches
* Exports
* Replicas
* Backups

# Tombstone

A minimal tombstone may remain after deletion to prevent:

* Reappearance from stale synchronization
* Duplicate ingestion
* Citation ambiguity
* Replay
* Retention-policy conflict

A tombstone should contain only necessary metadata and must not retain deleted content.

---

# Legal Hold

Legal hold may suspend ordinary deletion or alteration.

A hold should identify:

# Architectural Rules

Knowledge Architecture and Lifecycle must:

* Give every knowledge domain one accountable owner.
* Register sources before production use.
* Preserve stable source, artifact, item, and version identities.
* Keep source, artifact, item, and representation versions distinct.
* Preserve source authority, tenant, property, classification, and effective time.
* Treat acquired, processed, indexed, or embedded content as inactive until lifecycle eligibility is established.
* Assign explicit authority classes.
* Keep authority contextual to subject, scope, and time.
* Make published Knowledge-Item Versions immutable.
* Require a new version for material semantic change.
* Preserve provenance through every transformation.
* Distinguish canonical source content from derived content.
* Label AI-generated or AI-transformed knowledge as derived until governed approval.
* Preserve tenant and property scope in every lifecycle state and representation.
* Govern enterprise, tenant, and property inheritance and overrides explicitly.
* Separate approval from publication and publication from activation.
* Define required representations per publication channel.
* Exclude suspended, expired, withdrawn, and deleted content from current-use retrieval.
* Preserve authorized historical access to superseded versions where required.
* Propagate suspension, supersession, expiry, withdrawal, and deletion to every representation and cache.
* Keep the Knowledge Registry authoritative for knowledge lifecycle.
* Treat indexes as rebuildable projections.
* Keep citations version aware and lifecycle aware.
* Prevent silent redirection that changes historical evidence.
* Support correction through versioning, not silent mutation.
* Define retention separately for artifacts, items, representations, citations, and evidence.
* Use minimal tombstones only where necessary.
* Keep legal hold separate from current-use eligibility.
* Govern source withdrawal, tenant exit, and property transfer.
* Require authorized, attributable, idempotent lifecycle transitions.
* Reconcile lifecycle state across storage and retrieval systems.
* Produce evidence for review, approval, publication, activation, correction, supersession, withdrawal, archival, and deletion.
* Use versioned APIs and events.
* Prohibit direct cross-service database access.
* Remain vendor neutral and technology independent.

* Define one database or search engine
* Make Knowledge & Memory authoritative for domain-service facts

# Summary

The XeniosAI Knowledge Architecture and Lifecycle establishes the canonical model and governed states through which source information becomes controlled enterprise knowledge.

It defines knowledge domains, source and artifact identities, knowledge items, immutable versions, authority classes, tenant and property scope, temporal validity, provenance, lifecycle states, review, approval, publication, activation, correction, suspension, supersession, withdrawal, archival, deletion, and evidence.

The architecture makes publication explicit: acquisition, extraction, indexing, embedding, or prior use does not activate knowledge.

It also requires every lexical, semantic, graph, summary, translation, cache, and citation representation to follow the lifecycle of its source Knowledge-Item Version.

By keeping the Knowledge Registry authoritative for knowledge lifecycle while preserving domain-service ownership of business truth, XeniosAI can provide current, attributable, correctable, portable, and tenant-safe knowledge without turning search infrastructure into an uncontrolled system of record.
